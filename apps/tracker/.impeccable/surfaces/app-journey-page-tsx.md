# Research journey surface brief

- Scope: the public `/journey` route and its dedicated narrative, interactive stage, and stylesheet.
- Mode: Experience, with a readable scientific explanation available independently of the illustration.
- Audience and job: visitors and the researcher should understand the one-shot retrieval problem, the competing recipes, the proposed stress tests, and why every week contributes necessary evidence.
- Primary action: follow the natural page scroll; jump to a chapter or week, explore the model, then open the canonical plan or recorded weekly progress.
- Direction: a monochrome exhibition extending the incumbent identity. Large plain-language headings and an original synthetic 3D model share the viewport; the answer closes on a light field. This direction grants no global palette, font, or operational-page changes.
- Memorable moment: the same visual stage changes from articulated motion into a frozen representation, a similarity space, a damaged query beside a clean reference, and the structure of a locked comparison.
- Evidence: the 14 weekly narratives follow the canonical v3 calendar. Dates, states, required-task totals, and gates come from the existing public-plan loader, including its visible fallback notice; calendar position never implies completion.
- Constraints: original synthetic skeletons and conceptual geometry remain explicitly labeled. Do not present measured embeddings, trained checkpoints, accuracy results, licensed poses, or private owner/activity records through this route. The full-recipe comparison, clean gallery, sealed-test prerequisites, and positive/null/contrary outcomes remain legible in text.

## Boundaries for later revisions

Preserve keyboard access, visible focus, labeled ranges, native scrolling, and source-state clarity. Keep the clean reference distinct from the damaged query without relying on hue. Continue to treat scene layouts as explanations, not empirical evidence or current checkpoint status. Compact metadata sizes and decorative micro-labels are implementation details to review for legibility, not reusable design tokens. No unresolved product decision is introduced by this route.
