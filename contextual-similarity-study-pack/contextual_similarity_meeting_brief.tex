{\sffamily\bfseries\color{navy}\fontsize{20}{23}\selectfont Robust Pose-Sequence Retrieval with Contextual Metric Learning}\par
{\large Final-proposal discussion brief}\hfill{\small July 2026}
\vspace{2mm}\hrule height 1pt\vspace{3mm}

\begin{pitchbox}[title=\textbf{60-second pitch}]
I propose to transfer contextual metric learning from image retrieval to temporal pose embeddings. A pretrained motion encoder maps each skeleton sequence to a normalized vector, and cosine similarity ranks a clean reference gallery. The experimental question is whether optimizing \emph{neighborhood structure}, rather than only direct pairs, causes retrieval quality to degrade more slowly when query poses contain joint jitter, missing joints or limbs, dropped frames, or pose-estimator shift. I would hold the encoder, split, sampler, augmentations, and evaluator fixed; compare supervised contrastive, contextual, and hybrid objectives; and report Recall@1/5/10, mAP, and clean-to-corrupted degradation.
\end{pitchbox}

\begin{warnbox}[title=\textbf{Scientific boundary}]
The foundation paper establishes reduced overfitting and label-noise robustness for \emph{image retrieval}. It does not prove robustness to corrupted pose coordinates. That transfer is the hypothesis to test, not a premise to assume.
\end{warnbox}

\textbf{Five equations to narrate}
\begin{align*}
D(i,j)&=\lVert z_i-z_j\rVert_2^2=2-2s_{ij}.\\[-2pt]
N_{ij}&=\theta\!\left(-D(i,j)+\sg(D(i,p_k))+\epsilon\right),
\quad \frac{\partial\theta}{\partial x}=\alpha.\\[-2pt]
W_1(i,j)&=\frac{N_{ij}}{2}\left[\frac{M_+(i,j)}{\sg\sum_pN_{ip}}+\frac{M_-(i,j)}{\sg\sum_p(1-N_{ip})}\right].\\[-2pt]
W_2&=\frac{RW_1}{R\mathbf 1},\qquad W=\tfrac12(W_2+W_2^\top),\quad R=N_{k/2}\odot N_{k/2}^\top.\\[-2pt]
\mathcal L&=\lambda\,\frac{1}{n^2}\sum_{i\ne j}(y_{ij}-w_{ij})^2+(1-\lambda)\mathcal L_{\rm contrast}+\gamma\mathcal L_{\rm reg}.
\end{align*}

\begin{meetbox}[title=\textbf{The key theoretical statement}]
For a balanced batch with exactly $k$ samples per class, $n>2k$, and $\epsilon=0$, zero contextual loss occurs exactly when every positive ranks ahead of every negative within the batch. This is why $k$ is tied to samples per class rather than treated as an unrelated top-$k$ hyperparameter.
\end{meetbox}

\begin{implbox}[title=\textbf{Lowest-risk implementation sequence}]
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=1pt]
\item Freeze MotionBERT; validate splits, cosine retrieval, metrics, and deterministic corruptions.
\item Train only small projection heads with SupCon, contextual, and hybrid losses.
\item Profile contextual $O(n^3)$ batch operations; use balanced batches such as $16\times4=64$ or $32\times4=128$.
\item Fine-tune the full encoder only after the loss-side comparison is stable.
\item Add corruption-aware training and a MaskCLR-style robustness control.
\end{enumerate}
\end{implbox}

\textbf{Likely questions and precise answers}\par\vspace{1mm}
{\small
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.05}
\begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{>{\bfseries\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{0.29\textwidth}Y}
\toprule
Question & Answer \\
\midrule
Why retrieval rather than classification? & Classification can remain acceptable while the ordering of the embedding space deteriorates. Retrieval measures whether examples remain inspectable and useful for one-shot decisions. \\
Where is the novelty? & Not sequence retrieval itself. The bounded gap is whether the contextual objective improves \emph{retrieval degradation curves under input corruption} when the temporal encoder and evaluator are fixed. \\
Why might neighborhoods help? & A direct similarity can be unstable under local pose noise; shared and reciprocal neighborhoods provide a local-consensus signal. This is an inductive-bias hypothesis, not a guarantee. \\
Why NTU120 first? & It provides scale, many examples per class, standard protocols, and compatibility with existing skeleton and one-shot pipelines. It supports a reproducible first test, not claims about fine-grained technique quality. \\
What would a null result mean? & Robustness is mainly attributable to the encoder or corruption exposure rather than the contextual objective. That is still an informative controlled result. \\
What is the main engineering risk? & Cubic batch computation and batch-structure sensitivity. Both are bounded by moderate balanced batches, matrix operations, and staged profiling. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}}

\begin{warnbox}[title=\textbf{Do not claim}]
Do not claim that action-class retrieval measures movement quality, that label-noise robustness implies pose-noise robustness, or that a new motion architecture is being proposed. The contribution is a fair retrieval-first robustness test of the loss.
\end{warnbox}

\textbf{Closing sentence.} \emph{The project is deliberately narrow: it asks whether local neighborhood structure is a useful inductive bias when the observations, rather than the labels, become unreliable.}

\vfill{\footnotesize Foundation: Liao, Tsiligkaridis, and Kulis (ICML 2023). Proposed study: MotionBERT + NTU RGB+D 120 with controlled pose corruptions.}
